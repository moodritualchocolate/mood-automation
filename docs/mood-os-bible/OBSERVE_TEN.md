# OBSERVE_TEN — Ten humans, the ugly Day One, watch

The founder's experiment, not the lab version. We are not testing a theory. We are
testing whether people **want MOOD.** The build is intentionally ugly so that if
they respond, it's to the substance — not the art.

**Where it lives:** `/one` (Day 1) and `/one?day=2` (to observe Day 2 immediately).
Same Moment content as the real app, unstyled skin: system serif, black on white,
no images, no animation.

---

## The rule of the room

- **Do not explain.** Hand them the phone open to `/one`. Say only: *"Use this."*
- **Do not interview during use.** No hints, no reactions, no "what do you think?"
- **Observe silently.** You are watching a human meet MOOD with no help.

## What to watch (write it down live, one line each)

- Where do they **lean in**? Where do their eyes slow down?
- Where do they **hesitate** or look confused?
- Where do they get **bored** / try to skip / put the phone down?
- Do they **smile, pause, go quiet, react** — and exactly where?
- At the challenge: do they treat it as real? Do they say they'd actually do it?
- How long on the **recognition** lines — skimmed or absorbed?
- Do they reach the end, or drop off? Where?

## The five questions (only after they finish, in this order)

1. **What do you think MOOD is?**
2. **What do you remember?**
3. **What did you feel?**
4. **Would you come back tomorrow?**
5. **Why?**

Ask, then stay silent. Do not rescue a pause. Do not defend the product.

## The sixth thing — behavioral, not stated (this is the real answer to #4)

Stated intention lies. So we also **observe the actual return**:
- Send each person the `/one` link again **the next day, once**, plain: *"here's today's."*
- **Count who reopens it** without further nudging. That number — not their polite
  "yes" — is the truth about coming back. Day 2 shows them a genuinely new Moment
  (`moment_002`), so the return has something real to offer.

## Participants

- **10 people.** Not insiders. Do not tell them the vision, the category, or the word
  "becoming." They meet it cold, exactly as a stranger would.
- Mixed enough that a pattern means something (not all the same age/type).

## What we're reading in the answers

- **Q1 "what is it":** do they land somewhere true and interesting ("something that
  gets me to do a small real thing"), or somewhere dead ("a quiz," "a wellness app,"
  "I'm not sure")? Confusion here is a strong negative signal.
- **Q2 "remember":** do they remember a *feeling / the challenge / being seen* — or
  nothing specific? Remembering nothing = forgettable = fail.
- **Q3 "felt":** any real emotion at all, named unprompted, beats "it was fine."
- **Q4 + return:** the stated answer, *checked against* who actually reopens Day 2.

## Success (qualitative, honest — n=10 is a signal, not a proof)

- Most people describe MOOD as something **true and unusual**, not a known category.
- Most remember a **feeling or the moment they did/considered the challenge.**
- A clear majority say they'd return **and several actually reopen Day 2.**

## Failure

- People can't say what it is, or call it a wellness/journal/quiz app.
- They remember nothing specific, or only "it was nice / pretty" (and it isn't even
  pretty — so that answer means empty).
- They say "sure, maybe" to Q4 and **almost nobody reopens Day 2.**

## Founder decision matrix

- **Loved (want + return):** the substance carries even ugly. Build the beautiful
  version with confidence — art becomes an amplifier of a thing already wanted.
- **Warm but no return:** the moment lands, the *reason to come back* doesn't. Do not
  add polish yet — fix the return (the Becoming portrait, the open loop) and re-run
  the ugly test before spending on art.
- **Cold / confused:** the substance does not stand on its own. Beauty would only
  have hidden it. Stop, and rework what a Moment *is* before building anything.

*No art. No polish. Ten humans. Watch. Then five questions and one silent count.*
